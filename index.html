About me

I am a clinical psychology graduate from Iran. I finished first in my B.A. class at Payame Noor University, then completed my M.A. in Clinical Psychology at the University of Tabriz with a GPA of 18.27 out of 20.

My research asks why early adversity can leave people feeling disconnected for years, and which social and emotional processes carry that effect. Alongside research, I have trained in cognitive behavioural therapy and schema therapy.

The question behind my research

Adults who experienced abuse or neglect as children often report feeling lonelier. My thesis asked what carries that link.

I focused on two candidates. The first is social thermoregulation: how people use closeness with others to seek warmth and comfort. The second is risk avoidance: how strongly people steer away from risk. I tested whether these processes help explain why childhood trauma is followed by loneliness later in life.

I developed the proposal on my own and obtained ethics approval from the University of Tabriz Research Ethics Committee. I then collected questionnaire data, ran the analysis and wrote the introduction, method and discussion chapters.

For my PhD, I want to understand how early adversity shapes the ways people seek and avoid connection, and which processes protect people from chronic loneliness.

Research interests

  • Social thermoregulation

    The link between physical warmth and closeness with other people. My thesis tested it directly as a mediator.

  • Childhood trauma and its long-term effects

    How early abuse and neglect carry forward into adult relationships and wellbeing.

  • Loneliness and social connection

    Why some people struggle to feel connected, even when others are around.

  • Belonging and social needs

    The basic need to be accepted and to matter to others.

  • Nostalgia and emotion regulation

    How people draw on memories and feelings to cope with distress and disconnection.
